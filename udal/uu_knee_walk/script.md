## Fact check

| Claim in script | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| US study, people 50+ with knee osteoarthritis | Lo GH et al., "Association between walking for exercise and symptomatic and structural progression in individuals with knee osteoarthritis: data from the Osteoarthritis Initiative cohort", Arthritis Rheumatol 2022;74:1660-1667. OAI is NIH-sponsored, multicentre US cohort. | OK |
| "1,200 people" (screen 1,212) | 1,212 respondents (paper, via Relias Media / Hospital Healthcare Europe summaries) | OK |
| "followed for years" | OAI is a 10-year cohort; survey given at 96 months | OK (kept vague on purpose) |
| "40% less new daily (frequent) knee pain" | OR 0.6 (95% CI 0.4 to 0.8) for new frequent knee pain, walkers vs non-walkers | OK. Odds, not risk; "40% குறைவு" matches every press summary. |
| "not direct proof, observational" | Study is observational (self-reported walking history) | OK, honest caveat |
| "walking wears out the knee is false" | No evidence walking causes OA progression; the same study found 20% lower odds of joint-space narrowing (borderline CI 0.6 to 1.0), so we do NOT claim walking repairs the knee | OK, modest wording |
| Women most affected (description only) | WHO osteoarthritis fact sheet: 60% of people with OA are female, 73% older than 55; knee is the most affected joint | OK |

Rejected: "walking repairs cartilage" / "slows damage" (CI touches 1.0, preliminary).
