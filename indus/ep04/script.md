## Fact check
| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Priest-King" is a modern nickname; no inscription says priest or king | Confirmed (name from the excavators, by resemblance) | worldhistory.org; harappa.com |
| Statue: steatite, 17.5 cm, Mohenjo-daro, National Museum of Pakistan, Karachi | Confirmed (found 1925-27; year not said in voice because sources differ) | worldhistory.org image 12858 |
| No grand palaces or temples, no rich royal burials, no images of warfare or conquest, no monuments to rulers | Confirmed as the scholarly consensus; script says "we haven't found" | Strayer (Macmillan); harappa.com (Kenoyer) |
| Indus area larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined | Confirmed (approx. 1 million+ km2; often stated) | Durham Univ. "The Indus Valley Mystery"; Encyclopedia.com |
| Standard brick ratio 1:2:4 at sites hundreds of km apart | Confirmed (absolute sizes vary a little) | harappa.com; historyrise |
| Rule by elites (merchants, landowners, ritual specialists) not a single king: a theory | Kenoyer; script says "archaeologists still argue" | harappa.com |
| Unicorn on about 65-70% of seals; no other civilisation of the time used it | Confirmed | harappa.com, Kenoyer "Iconography of the Indus Unicorn" |
Care: "no clear evidence of kings" is an interpretation; the script only says what has NOT been found and that experts argue.
