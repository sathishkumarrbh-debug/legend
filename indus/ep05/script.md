## Fact check
| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Unicorn = most common Indus seal motif, ~65-70% of seals across sites (over 60% at Mohenjo-daro) | Confirmed; voice says "nearly seven of every ten" | harappa.com (Kenoyer, "Iconography of the Indus Unicorn"; "Unicorn sealings and seals") |
| Earliest unicorn on seals c. 2600 BC; not reported from any other contemporaneous civilisation | Confirmed (Kenoyer); voice: "no other civilisation of its time" | harappa.com |
| Unicorn seals reached Mesopotamia (Kish) | Confirmed: Mackay's Kish seal (Wikimedia, public domain: "Indus Valley unicorn seal and etched carnelian beads found in Kish") | Wikimedia Commons; harappa.com |
| Debate: one-horned animal (Kenoyer, from terracotta one-horned figurines) vs bull in profile / artistic convention (Marshall, Possehl) | Confirmed: script gives both sides | harappa.com "One or two unicorn horns"; Atre |
| Object in front: incense burner (Marshall), sacred filter (Mahadevan), feeding trough etc.; unresolved | Confirmed | harappa.com; Mahadevan 1993 |
| Unicorn motif disappears c. 1900 BC with the script | Confirmed (Kenoyer) | harappa.com |
| Ctesias of Cnidus, Greek physician at the Persian court, c. 400 BC, described one-horned wild asses of India; never visited India | Confirmed ("1,500 years later" ≈ 1900 BC → 400 BC) | worldhistory.org "The Unicorn Myth"; Photius' summary |
| Most experts think Ctesias described the Indian rhinoceros | Confirmed as the main theory; the Indus link is asked as a question, not claimed | worldhistory.org; Gizmodo |
| A rare seal shows a seated figure surrounded by animals ("Pashupati" seal) | Confirmed: teaser for Part 6 | Marshall 1931; harappa.com |
