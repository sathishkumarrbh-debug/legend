## Description
The legendary Damascus swords were forged from wootz, a crucible steel made in South India. Smiths sealed iron and plant matter in small clay pots and fired them for hours, producing cakes of high-carbon steel that traders carried to Persia and Syria. There, smiths forged them into blades with the famous flowing "watered" pattern.

By the 1800s the traditional furnaces had gone cold and the exact method was lost. Michael Faraday studied wootz in 1819 while trying to reproduce it. In 2006, scientists reported carbon nanotubes and cementite nanowires in a 17th-century Damascus sabre (Reibold et al., Nature 444, 286).

The silk-cutting story is a legend, not a recorded test. The origin of the word "wootz" is debated; it is usually traced to South Indian words for steel such as ukku / urukku.
