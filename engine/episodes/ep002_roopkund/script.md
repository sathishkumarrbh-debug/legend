# EP002: The Lake of Skeletons (Roopkund, Uttarakhand)

**Target:** 50 to 55 seconds of speech  |  **Read:** one line, short pause, next line. Fast, like telling a friend.

## Script (13 lines)

1. High in the Himalayas, there's a lake full of human skeletons.
2. Hundreds of them. And for decades, nobody knew who they were.
3. This is Roopkund, five thousand metres up in Uttarakhand.
4. In 1942, a forest ranger found the bones as the ice melted.
5. The first fear? Japanese soldiers from World War Two.
6. Wrong. These bones were far older.
7. Then came the legend: pilgrims killed by a deadly hailstorm.
8. But in 2019, scientists tested their DNA, and the story broke apart.
9. Most were Indians who died more than a thousand years ago.
10. But fourteen of them were different. Their DNA matched people from Greece and Crete.
11. And they died around 1800. A thousand years later.
12. What were Mediterranean travellers doing on a frozen Himalayan lake?
13. Nobody knows. And every summer, the ice gives back a few more bones...

## Delivery notes
- Line 1: slow, heavy. Let "skeletons" land.
- Line 6: one sharp word, "Wrong.", then pause.
- Line 10: slow down on "Greece and Crete". That's the twist.
- Line 13: almost a whisper at the end.

## Description
Roopkund, a glacial lake at about 5,000 m in Uttarakhand, is scattered with the remains of hundreds of people. A 2019 ancient-DNA study found at least three groups: people of South Asian ancestry who died between the 7th and 10th centuries, and a group with eastern Mediterranean ancestry who died around 1800. How they got there is still a mystery.

Sources: Harney et al., Nature Communications 10, 3670 (2019); Max Planck Institute press release (2019).
