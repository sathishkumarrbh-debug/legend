TOPIC: Bodhidharma, the monk credited with bringing Chan (Zen) Buddhism to China, and the Japanese Daruma doll that is based on him.

KEY FACTS I have (please verify):
- Lived around the 5th to 6th century CE.
- Earliest source, Yang Xuanzhi (547 CE), calls him a monk from the Western Regions, "a Persian Central Asian".
- Tanlin's preface (6th century) says he was the third son of a great king in South India; Daoxuan (7th century) says South Indian Brahmin stock.
- A later Indian/Tamil tradition links him to the Pallava kings of Kanchipuram.
- Legend: he meditated facing a wall for nine years at or near Shaolin; later legend says his legs withered, which is why the Daruma doll has no legs.
- Legend: he taught the Shaolin monks exercises that became kung fu. Historians reject this; it comes from a 17th-century text (Yijin Jing) and was popularised in the 20th century.
- Legend: he cut off his eyelids to stay awake and tea plants grew from them.
- Japanese Daruma dolls: you paint in one eye when you set a goal, and the other when you achieve it.
