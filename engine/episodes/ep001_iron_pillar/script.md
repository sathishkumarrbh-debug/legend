# EP001: The Pillar That Refuses to Rust (v3, story + pride)
**Series:** Pride of India, Episode 1  |  **Target:** 50 to 55 seconds  |  Warm, proud, storytelling voice

## How to record (new: takes are fine)
- Read each line, pause a second, go to the next.
- **If a line doesn't feel right, just read it again straight away.** Read any line 2 or 3 times.
  You can also read the whole script 2 or 3 times in separate files. The editor listens to every take
  and keeps the best one of each line automatically.
- Speak like you're telling a friend something amazing, not reading. Smile on the last two lines.

## Script (14 lines, v4 after ChatGPT + Gemini review)
1. Sixteen hundred years out in the open, and this iron pillar has barely rusted.
2. No factory. No machines. Just fire and human hands.
3. It was made around 400 CE, in the time of the Gupta emperor Chandragupta the Second.
4. Over six tonnes of iron, hammered together piece by piece.
5. But here's the twist. It wasn't made for Delhi.
6. Historians believe it first stood at Udayagiri, in Madhya Pradesh.
7. Centuries later, a cannonball slammed into it. The dent is still there.
8. It didn't break. It didn't fall.
9. So what's the secret?
10. It's in the iron itself.
11. Its high phosphorus helped grow a protective layer, about as thin as a human hair.
12. Scientists have studied it for over a hundred years.
13. Built by Indian hands, sixteen centuries ago. And still standing tall.
14. That's the pride of India.

## Delivery
- Line 1: slow and heavy, like a movie trailer.
- Line 5: three beats, "fire... hammers... human hands".
- Lines 6 and 10: drop your voice, like a secret.
- Line 9: two punches.
- Lines 13 and 14: warm and proud, a little slower.

## Fact check
| Claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| About 1,600 years, around 400 CE, Chandragupta II | Inscription + mainstream dating (Wikipedia, Britannica) |
| Over six tonnes, one pillar | Standard figure; forge-welded from hot iron lumps by hand (Balasubramaniam) |
| First stood at Udayagiri | Majority scholarly view, phrased "historians believe" |
| Cannonball dent | Dent is documented; attacker not named on purpose |
| Phosphorus-rich iron, protective layer about 0.05 mm (a hair is about 0.07 mm) | Balasubramaniam, IIT Kanpur |
| Still studied today | Ongoing metallurgy research |
