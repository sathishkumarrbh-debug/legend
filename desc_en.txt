The Kailasa temple (Cave 16) at Ellora, Maharashtra, was not built from blocks. Around 1,250 years ago, workers cut three deep trenches into a basalt hillside, leaving one giant block, and then carved it from the top down: the tower first, the floor last. Inside they left a two-storey temple, life-size stone elephants and a stone bridge. No inscription on the temple names its builder; copper plates written decades later (812 CE) credit the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I.
